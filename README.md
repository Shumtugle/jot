# Jot

A photo annotator for Android. Open a picture, draw on it, crop it, put a caption on it, save it — and everything you put on the photo lies on the same sheet as the photo itself.

The governing idea is that a photograph is a drawing on rubber. A stroke, a caption, a shape, an inserted picture — all of them lie on that rubber and stretch with it. Mirror the sheet and the caption mirrors too: unreadable is the answer, not a defect. Three things are deliberately not rubber: the checkerboard table the sheet lies on, the guide lines held beside it, and the crop frame — those are scissors, not drawing.

Every line of it was generated by AI through plain conversation. No Android Studio, no Gradle: a WebView shell around one self-contained HTML page, assembled by hand and signed with `uber-apk-signer`.

**Package:** `io.github.shumtugle.jot` · **Site:** [shumtugle.github.io](https://shumtugle.github.io/)

## What it does

- **Draw, cut, fill.** Brush and marker, cut-out by shape or by colour, soft edges, fill of what was cut away.
- **Correction and glass.** Brightness, contrast, saturation and the rest; blur, sharpen, focus, tilt, aberration, frames.
- **Crop and turn.** Free or fixed ratio, quarter turns, mirror, fine angle.
- **Captions and shapes** with a full font stack, laid on the sheet like everything else.
- **Fourteen languages**, right-to-left included.
- **Session memory.** Close the app mid-work and the work is still there.

## How it is built

The page is a straight concatenation — `src/shell.html` plus the scripts, in an order that matters — and the result is dropped into the APK as `assets/index.html`. Nothing is compiled. A release is a swap of two HTML files inside a zip and four numbers kept in agreement.

```
sh src/build.sh
```

## What holds it together

A test bench of 22 suites and over 2350 checks, run before every release. Zero failures is a condition of building, not a wish.

The checks are unusual in one way: most of them guard *reasons*, not just behaviour. A silent `catch` without a stated reason fails the bench. A number that lives in two places fails the bench. A rule whose subject no longer exists fails the bench — because a rule without a subject will eventually be read as if it still applied.

## Trace

The app keeps a short trace of what changed the state of things: screen entered, tool taken, edit made, step back, open, save. It is readable from settings. Messages are keyed, not spelled out at the point of failure, so the trace speaks one language and the code carries none.
