The governing idea

A photograph is a drawing on rubber. A stroke, a caption, a shape, an inserted picture — all of them lie on that rubber and stretch with it.

Mirror the sheet and the caption mirrors too. Unreadable is the answer, not a defect.

Three things are deliberately not rubber: the checkerboard table the sheet lies on, the guide lines held beside it, and the crop frame. Those are scissors, not drawing.

on the same sheet

Drag the slider. The caption is not an overlay — it deforms because it is part of the sheet. Mirror it and it becomes unreadable, which is the correct result.

The tool sheet

Twelve tools, plus open, save, share and undo on the row beneath them.

What it does

  • Crop and turnFree or fixed ratio, quarter turns, mirror, fine angle.
  • Captions and shapesA full font stack, laid on the sheet like everything else.
  • Fourteen languagesRight-to-left included.
  • Session memoryClose the app mid-work and the work is still there.

How it is built

No Android Studio, no Gradle. A WebView shell wrapped around one self-contained HTML page: the page is a straight concatenation of a shell and the scripts, in an order that matters, dropped into the APK as an asset. Nothing is compiled. A release is a swap of two HTML files inside a zip and four numbers kept in agreement.

Every line was generated by AI through plain conversation. No human wrote one.

What holds it together

A bench of 22 suites and more than 2350 checks runs before every release, and zero failures is a condition of building rather than a wish.

The checks are unusual in one way: most of them guard reasons, not just behaviour. A silent catch without a stated reason fails the bench. A number that lives in two places fails the bench. A rule whose subject no longer exists fails the bench — because a rule without a subject will eventually be read as if it still applied.
