Jot — a photo annotator for Android

Open a picture, draw on it, cut a piece out, put a caption with a tail on it, save it. Everything you add lies on the same sheet as the photograph and stretches with it.

  • Package io.github.shumtugle.jot
  • Languages 14, right-to-left included
  • Permissions none
  • Network never

The idea it is built on

A photograph is a drawing on rubber. A stroke, a caption, a shape, an inserted picture — all of them lie on that rubber. Stretch it and they stretch. Mirror it and the caption mirrors too: unreadable is the answer, not a defect.

Three things are deliberately not rubber. The checkerboard table is the room the sheet lies in. The guide lines are a ruler held beside the sheet — they stay on the axes while the sheet tilts, or there would be nothing left to straighten by. The crop frame is scissors, not drawing.

What it does

  • Draw and cut. Brush and marker, cut-out by shape or by colour, soft edges, fill of what was taken away.
  • Correct. Nine quantities on one slider, and a colour mixer: eight stretches of the hue wheel, each with its own hue, saturation and lightness. Touch the greenery without touching the brickwork.
  • Glass. Blur, sharpen, focus, tilt, aberration, frames.
  • Crop and turn. Free or fixed ratio, quarter turns, mirror, fine angle — fitted together rather than one after another.
  • Captions and shapes with a full font stack, laid on the sheet like everything else.
  • Session memory. Close the app mid-work and the work is still there.
  • Landscape. The panels stand in a column at the right and the picture takes the whole left side — but only where that actually helps. On a folding phone opened flat, a column would take more from the picture than the bars below it, so the bars stay.

How it is built

No Android Studio, no Gradle, nothing compiled. Jot is a WebView shell around one self-contained HTML page. A release is a swap of two files inside a zip and four numbers kept in agreement.

sh src/build.sh     # concatenate the page
node tools/count.js # run the bench

Every line of it was generated by AI through plain conversation. Not a single line was written by a human hand.

What holds it together

A bench of 22 suites and over 2360 checks, run before every release. Zero failures is a condition of building, not a wish.

The checks are unusual in one way: most of them guard reasons rather than behaviour alone.

A silent catch without a stated reason fails the bench. A number living in two places fails the bench. A rule whose subject no longer exists fails the bench — because a rule without a subject will eventually be read as if it still applied.

Reading the code is not enough and the bench knows it. Before any release the page is loaded whole and asked whether every button in it got dressed: a dead build passes two thousand static checks and opens on the phone with blank buttons. That has happened, which is why the step exists.

Trace

The app keeps a short trace of what changed the state of things: screen entered, tool taken, edit made, step back, open, save. It is readable from the settings, behind the beta switch. Messages are keyed rather than spelled out at the point of failure, so the trace speaks one language and the code carries none.
